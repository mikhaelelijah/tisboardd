Squad Fox’s Scientific and Spiritual Journey at Eagle Hills

Megamendung, Bogor — What happens when young minds step outside the classroom and into the heart of nature? For Squad Fox—a team of four dedicated students from Teach Indonesia School—the Adventure Camp 2026 became an immersive expedition blending rigorous science with profound spiritual reflection.

Set against the misty, serene backdrop of Eagle Hills in Megamendung, their journey titled "Nature’s Symphony: An Observation Journey" offers a powerful look at how ecosystems function and why humanity holds a sacred duty to protect them.

The Abiotic Foundation: Stones and Streams

Every thriving ecosystem begins with its non-living elements. Squad Fox members Mikhael Elijah and Jessica Abigail kicked off their field research by examining the fundamental physical pillars of the landscape:

  • Milky Quartz: Holding a piece of Milky Quartz by the riverbank, Mikhael noted that its distinct cloudiness carries a history of ancient water and gas trapped deep within the silica. With a hardness of 7 on the Mohs scale, these resilient stones symbolize the enduring strength of the Earth.
  • The Ciliwung River: Tracing the upstream flow, Jessica highlighted the river’s dendritic pattern, which acts as a primary artery for the region. Through fluvial erosion, rushing waters have smoothed the river stones over years, vividly demonstrating the raw shaping power of water on the abiotic world.

The Biotic Tapestry: Flora and Fauna

Moving from the earth to living organisms, Yeeremiah Sebachtian Giarso, Benn Daniel Wong, and Mikhael dove into the rich biodiversity of the highland forest:

  • The Towering Rasamala (Altingia excelsa): These forest giants can reach staggering heights of 60 meters. They play a critical role in carbon sequestration, helping the planet breathe, while their natural balsam provides built-in durability.
  • Pollinators and Flowering Plants: Bright blooms like the Hibiscus and Allamanda serve as biological beacons. By supplying nectar, they attract vital pollinators like bees and butterflies, ensuring the continuation of plant generations.
  • The Orthoptera Family: Down in the grass, the team discovered grasshoppers and crickets. Fascinatingly, these creatures hear through their legs and act as essential primary consumers, maintaining the delicate energy flow within the highland food web.

A Call to Environmental Stewardship

Beyond scientific classification, the camp experience challenged the students to look at creation through a moral and spiritual lens. Observing the seamless interdependence of insects, trees, and rivers led to a profound realization: balance requires respect.

"The Lord God took the man and put him in the Garden of Eden to work it and take care of it." -- Genesis 2:15

Building on this, Benn emphasized that humanity's role in Megamendung is not that of mere visitors, but of active guardians. The group issued a clear, actionable challenge to everyone: be mindful of waste, protect our forests, and cherish the nature around us.
