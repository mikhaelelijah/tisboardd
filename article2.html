Rethinking the Core Purpose of Schools: Community Over Curriculum

Mikhael

Published on June 15

Recent discussions have highlighted a need to distinguish between learning, education, and schooling. While schools generally operate under the mandate of facilitating learning, they often measure it through a very narrow set of skills and disciplines. The reality is that learning happens constantly, with or without a physical school building.

As we reflect on the role of education, we must ask: Are we forgetting the true, unmatched advantage of a physical school?

The Limitations of the Online Shift

As educational systems explore permanent remote learning options, many parents are discovering the benefits long enjoyed by homeschooling families:

Homeschooling Benefits:

  • Less time spent commuting
  • More quality time spent as a family
  • Greater student autonomy over their daily schedules
  • Increased efficiency and focus in completing tasks

While online learning undeniably offers efficiency, focusing purely on academic delivery misses the broader, more vital purpose of a school.

The True Value of School: Connection and Belonging

Educator Pernille Ripp highlights that the essence of school is not merely academics, but community, connection, and belonging. When asked what they miss about being away from the classroom, students rarely say they miss the curriculum—they miss being together.

Learning occurred for centuries before modern schools existed. What public schools uniquely provide is a democratic space where diverse individuals gather. This environment offers a powerful opportunity by:

A Powerful Opportunity:

  • Fostering Belonging: Creating equitable spaces where every individual feels seen, heard, and valued.
  • Challenging Bias: Bringing together varied cultures, races, and beliefs into one shared space, offering a natural setting to tackle racism and prejudice head-on.
  • Embracing Diversity: Treating the inherent diversity of a public school as a profound gift and a vital learning tool, rather than just a demographic metric.

Prioritizing the Irreplaceable

As schools plan for face-to-face instruction, the focus should shift toward experiences that cannot be replicated on a computer screen. Instead of prioritizing traditional academic rankings, schools should emphasize shared, creative endeavors.

Educational expert Gary Stager points out that futurists often overlook the very subjects that give physical schools their true value: music, art, and drama. For many students, participating in a school band, playing sports, or engaging in a theater production is the primary reason they want to attend school in person. Schools must stop undervaluing these deeply human, collaborative experiences.

The Bottom Line

While improving academic opportunities will always be a priority, schools have a much higher obligation: cultivating great communities. The ultimate goal of a physical school is not just to provide a place to learn, but to provide a space for students to simply be—and to be together.
